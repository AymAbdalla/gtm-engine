# Angles

An angle is the reason a message gives for writing. Use one angle per draft and tag it, so you can later see which angles get replies.

Default list (`copy.angles` in `gtm.json`; add or remove freely):

| Tag | The message says | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `trigger` | "I saw this dated event, and it often leads to this problem." | First touch with a strong signal. |
| `peer_proof` | "Teams like yours got this result." One VERIFIED proof line. | First touch when the proof fits the persona and company size. |
| `problem_question` | One sharp question about how they handle a known problem today. | First touch with a weak or no signal. |
| `cost_of_waiting` | What the problem costs if nothing changes, in plain words, no invented numbers. | Second or third touch. |
| `smaller_ask` | A yes or no question instead of a meeting. | After no reply to a meeting ask. |
| `referral` | "Who owns this at your company?" | When the role fit is unsure. |
| `close_the_loop` | A short, polite last note that makes it easy to say "not now". | Last step. |

## Rotation

- In one batch, vary first-touch angles. The checker notes it when three or more first touches all use the same angle.
- For the same person, do not repeat an angle on the same channel within a sequence.
- When you change the copy of an angle, bump the sequence `version` so old and new results stay apart.

## Judging angles

Wait for enough first touches per angle before calling a winner (a review after about 30 first touches per angle is a sensible default). Keep, rewrite once, or cut. Small numbers are anecdotes, not rates.
